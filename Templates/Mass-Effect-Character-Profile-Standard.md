Mass Effect Individual Character Profile Standard

Status: Established
Scope: Individual Mass Effect characters, excluding user-created original characters who have their own Omniversal counterparts and governing profiles.
Structure: 55 sections organized into 12 categories.
Purpose: Reusable profile standard and template; this document is not itself an individual character profile.

────────

1. Purpose and scope

This standard defines the information architecture and documentation rules for individual Mass Effect character profiles. It applies to major and minor characters, including those with limited official appearances or information.

The standard is comprehensive, but not every character will have meaningful information for every section. Do not invent information to fill gaps. Use these terms precisely:

• Unknown: The information has not been discovered or established.
• Undefined: The framework deliberately leaves the matter open.
• Not Applicable: The subject does not apply to this character.
• Unconfirmed: A claim or possibility exists but has not been verified.

2. Canon and documentation principles

Source classification

Distinguish information as:

• Canon: Established by official Mass Effect material.
• Framework Addition: Deliberately established by this project to fill a plausible gap or define how the framework operates.
• Interpretation: A reasoned reading of existing material that is not explicitly confirmed by canon.

Do not present framework additions or interpretations as official canon.

Source hierarchy and attribution

• Games and their player-dependent outcomes take priority where supplementary sources conflict.
• Official supplementary material may be used where it does not conflict with stronger in-game evidence.
• Cite sources alongside the information they support, not solely in an end bibliography.
• Distinguish direct appearances from mentions, references, codex entries, and other indirect appearances.
• Record unresolved conflicts rather than forcing an unsupported reconciliation.
• New official material may supersede earlier assumptions or framework additions where appropriate.

Player-dependent continuity

There is no universal default playthrough. Account for applicable player-dependent outcomes, including recruitment, loyalty, relationships, romance, survival, deaths, faction choices, political decisions, the Genophage, Geth–Quarian outcomes, the Collector Base, and Reaper War decisions. Do not silently collapse mutually exclusive outcomes into one definitive playthrough.

Composite timeline principle

For an Omniversal character, all applicable timeline versions are considered true from that character’s own point of view.

• The character subjectively remembers and experiences applicable events across different timeline versions.
• If a branch results in the character’s death, the Omniversal representation experiences the surviving counterpart’s outcome instead.
• Different characters may remember the same event differently and may meet to discuss those differing experiences.
• A character’s account is genuine to their experience, but may be incomplete or unreliable; this does not necessarily mean deliberate deception.
• No single playthrough or timeline is declared the objective winner.
• The objective mechanics by which these timelines coexist remain undefined unless separately established.

This principle applies to the character’s Omniversal representation and the Omniversal Realm. It does not rewrite events within the Mass Effect universe.

3. Omniversal rules relevant to Mass Effect characters

• Mass Effect characters may appear in the shared Omniversal Realm.
• How a character enters the Realm, who brings them, and whether independent access is possible remain undefined unless established individually.
• Each character’s entry point is individual; entry does not automatically occur at the ending of Mass Effect 3.
• If a character dies in the Mass Effect universe, they may still exist and participate in the Omniversal Realm, but cannot return to the Mass Effect universe. They know that they died.
• If that character subsequently dies in the Omniversal Realm, that death is final.
• Synthetic, technological, and exceptional cases are considered individually. Resurrection, restoration, backup recovery, and reconstruction are neither automatically permitted nor automatically prohibited.
• Mass Effect characters may interact with characters from other franchises in the shared Omniversal Realm, but universe-specific characters do not enter other franchise universes.
• Character autonomy remains intact. Friendship, loyalty, employment, or crew membership does not make a character automatically obedient to Azrael.
• Do not assume a character knows about the Omniversal Realm or its rules unless their individual history establishes that knowledge.
• Changes to memories, equipment, affiliations, knowledge, relationships, or status must be documented and justified.

4. Framework-wide ability, equipment, and species rules

These rules define available source pools and baseline mechanics. They do not automatically grant every possible ability or item to every individual.

Abilities

• All biotics have access to Barrier. Individual proficiency, application, and effectiveness vary.
• All Geth have access to Hunter Mode, drawing from the Geth units represented in Mass Effect 3 multiplayer.
• Ability lists may draw from combat, biotic, and tech abilities across all Mass Effect games and multiplayer.
• An ability’s availability in the framework does not establish that a particular character possesses it. Individual access must be supported by canon, a justified framework addition, or a clearly marked interpretation.

Equipment

• The framework may draw from known Mass Effect weapons and armor across the games and multiplayer.
• Equipment must be compatible with the chronology and technology available by the Mass Effect 3 Reaper War.
• Andromeda-origin equipment is excluded unless it is of Milky Way origin and otherwise chronologically compatible.
• Items that could not have appeared during the Reaper War must not be assigned as Reaper War equipment.
• Possession, access, authorization, familiarity, operation, maintenance, repair, modification, and manufacture are distinct capabilities.

Species and constructs

• The framework may include all species established in Mass Effect lore, including major, minor, playable, non-playable, extinct, codex-only, and supplementary-source species.
• Geth have a separate species framework.
• Alliance Infiltration Units, including the multiplayer representation associated with EDI-like infiltration units, have their own profile category.
• Other constructs should be documented with their associated character or construct profile as appropriate.
• Species-wide information belongs in the relevant species framework. Individual profiles document how those characteristics apply to the particular character.
• Do not infer personality, beliefs, competence, or behavior from species membership alone.

Biology and compatibility

• Preserve established species biology, culture, technology, law, military, religion, language, economy, civilian life, and history.
• Unknown biological or reproductive compatibility between species remains undefined; a relationship does not establish compatibility.
• Medical care, interspecies facilities, and treatment must remain consistent with established biology and technology.
• Individual knowledge must be limited to what the character could reasonably know. Classified or restricted information is not automatically available to them.

5. Individual profile structure

The following 55 sections are grouped into 12 categories for navigation. Section numbers are stable identifiers and should be retained for cross-referencing.

Category I — Identity and Personal Background

1. Identity

Name, aliases, titles, species, gender, age, birthplace, nationality or citizenship where applicable, and other identifying details.

2. Appearance

Physical appearance, body type, distinguishing features, clothing and presentation, markings, and notable changes in appearance.

3. Background

General biographical context, upbringing, formative circumstances, and relevant personal history not better documented in a more specific section.

30. Family and Ancestry

Parents, guardians, siblings, children, extended family, ancestry, lineage, descendants, family traditions, family dynamics, and family status.

23. Cultural and Social Background

Upbringing, cultural influences, traditions, social environment, values, norms, social identity, cultural development, and departures from cultural expectations. Do not stereotype individuals based on species or culture.

36. Species-Specific Characteristics

How species-wide biological or synthetic characteristics apply to the individual: physiology, adaptations, senses, lifespan, reproduction, environmental needs, individual variations, and limitations. Keep species-wide reference material in the species framework.

Category II — Personality and Psychology

4. Personality

General temperament, personality traits, disposition, and typical interpersonal presentation.

5. Psychological Profile

Internal motivations, fears, trauma, coping mechanisms, emotional triggers, psychological vulnerabilities, resilience, and relevant internal psychological conditions.

33. Psychological and Behavioral Patterns

Observable recurring behavior, decision-making patterns, conflict responses, interpersonal behavior, stress responses, habits, risk tolerance, contradictions, and behavioral development.

15. Character Development

Overall progression and changes over time, including changes in personality, outlook, capabilities, relationships, and circumstances.

40. Character Arc and Major Turning Points

Defining experiences, pivotal decisions, turning points, worldview changes, personal transformations, affected relationships, setbacks, recoveries, branching arcs, and Omniversal development. Distinguish the arc’s meaning from the chronological record of events.

37. Personal Goals and Future Aspirations

Immediate and long-term goals, unfinished objectives, personal desires, collective goals, obstacles, changing priorities, Omniversal goals, and goal status. Distinguish goals from underlying motivations.

Category III — History and Experiences

6. Timeline and History

Chronological life history, major events, relevant dates, and the character’s composite timeline where applicable.

7. Notable Events and Appearances

Official media appearances and significant events, including conditional appearances. Distinguish direct appearances from mentions or indirect references. Include source citations alongside relevant information.

17. Survival, Death, and Resurrection History

Near-death experiences, apparent and confirmed deaths, resurrections, reconstruction, survival mechanisms, lasting consequences, timeline variants, and current status. Document Mass Effect-universe history here; apply Omniversal mortality rules separately under Omniversal Status.

55. Travel and Exploration History

Significant personal journeys, destinations, exploration, expeditions, discoveries, cultural exposure, travel circumstances, environmental experiences, personal impact, and unfinished journeys. Includes civilian and personal travel, not only assigned operations.

42. Operational History and Mission Record

Military operations, assignments, missions, intelligence operations, expeditions, command responsibilities, outcomes, collaborators, classified operations, and operational progression. Distinguish this from the complete life timeline and general combat capability.

Category IV — Capabilities and Expertise

8. Abilities

What the character can do, including combat, biotic, tech, and other special abilities. Identify the source and basis for individual access.

9. Skills

Learned competencies and practical proficiencies. Distinguish demonstrated skill from innate ability, potential, or mere familiarity.

27. Physical Capabilities and Limitations

Strength, speed, agility, endurance, reflexes, senses, environmental tolerance, physical performance, aging, physiology, and physical limits. Do not use this as a substitute for combat or skill documentation.

21. Combat Profile

Fighting style, battlefield role, tactics, strengths and weaknesses in combat, combat experience, adaptability, and combat teamwork. Distinguish what the character can do from how they fight.

34. Biotic Profile

Biotic potential, specialization, techniques, Barrier access and proficiency, amp type and compatibility, training, limitations, and development. Required for every character; mark Not Applicable for non-biotics. All biotics have Barrier access under this framework.

35. Technological Profile

Technical expertise, omni-tools, cybernetics and implants, hacking, electronic warfare, AI/VI interaction, engineering, drones, combat platforms, scanners, technical limitations, and development. Distinguish possessing technology from understanding, operating, repairing, modifying, or manufacturing it.

47. Scientific and Technical Knowledge Profile

Scientific disciplines, research, specialization, experimentation, discoveries, methodology, academic credentials, collaborations, applied science, and limitations. Distinguish scientific knowledge and research from practical technological operation.

32. Education, Training, and Instruction

Formal education, military, technical, combat, biotic, intelligence, diplomatic, and other specialized training; apprenticeships, mentors, self-education, qualifications, and progression. Distinguish training from demonstrated current skill.

49. Environmental Adaptation and Survival Profile

Wilderness survival, hostile-environment adaptation, exploration, survival training, emergency preparation, improvisation, hazard recognition, infrastructure independence, and limitations. Species adaptation does not automatically establish survival skill.

50. Transportation and Piloting Profile

Spacecraft, ground vehicles, aircraft, navigation, handling, certifications, transportation systems, specialization, experience, and limitations. Distinguish piloting skill from vehicle ownership or access.

Category V — Professional and Operational Life

22. Professional and Civilian Life

Occupation, career, employment, professional expertise, civilian activities, living circumstances, workplace relationships, and general professional or civilian context.

43. Leadership and Command Profile

Leadership style, command experience, decision-making, delegation, personnel management, command relationships, crisis leadership, strategic planning, subordinate effectiveness, and limitations. Rank or position alone does not establish competence.

44. Logistics and Resource Management

Supply management, ammunition and equipment, maintenance, transportation, provisioning, resource allocation, supply chains, operational logistics, expertise, and constraints. Knowledge of resources does not imply authority or access.

45. Intelligence and Information Profile

Intelligence gathering, reconnaissance, analysis, counterintelligence, interrogation, information security, source networks, threat assessment, reliability, and limitations. Distinguish what the character knows from how information is obtained and assessed.

46. Diplomatic and Negotiation Profile

Negotiation, diplomacy, persuasion, mediation, conflict resolution, political interactions, intercultural diplomacy, negotiation style, relationships, and limitations. Diplomatic ability is not automatically implied by rank or position.

Category VI — Relationships and Social Life

12. Relationships

Detailed interpersonal connections, including friendship, romance, family, rivalry, professional, political, diplomatic, and loyalty relationships. Document individual relationships and their histories here; use focused sections for family, romance, and the wider social network.

31. Romantic and Intimate History

Current and former romances, attractions, marriage or partnership, intimate history where relevant, relationship development, emotional significance, player-dependent outcomes, and status. The composite timeline principle allows mutually exclusive romance outcomes to coexist in the Omniversal representation.

53. Social Network and Personal Connections

The broader network surrounding the character: friendships, acquaintances, professional contacts, mentors, informants, sources, rivals, social circles, network influence, reliability, and development. Describes the network as a whole rather than replacing detailed individual relationship histories.

41. Interactions with Other Species and Cultures

Cross-species relationships, cultural understanding and misunderstandings, diplomacy, prejudice, adaptation, individual attitudes, cross-cultural experiences, and influence. Keep this individual-specific and avoid species stereotypes.

48. Creative, Artistic, and Recreational Profile

Artistic abilities, creative interests, hobbies, recreation, entertainment preferences, expression, social recreation, skill development, and personal significance. Distinguish talent from interest.

Category VII — Beliefs and Personal Principles

24. Political and Ideological Views

Political beliefs, ideology, political loyalties, positions on major conflicts and institutions, political moral positions, involvement, and evolution of beliefs.

51. Ethics and Personal Moral Code

Core moral principles, boundaries, use of force, compromise, treatment of others, justice, moral dilemmas, consistency between words and deeds, and moral development. A coherent moral code does not automatically make a character morally good.

52. Religious, Spiritual, and Philosophical Beliefs

Religious affiliation, faith, spirituality, existential beliefs, views on life and death, creation and origins, synthetic existence, personal philosophy, practices, and belief development. Do not assume beliefs based on species, culture, or government. Atheism, agnosticism, skepticism, and absence of belief are valid possibilities.

Category VIII — Equipment, Property, and Resources

10. Equipment

Weapons, armor, gear, tools, and operational equipment. Record relevant provenance, availability, compatibility, and chronology. Distinguish equipment from general assets and sentimental belongings.

25. Resources and Personal Assets

Wealth, finances, property, businesses, vehicles, possessions, support networks, resource access, and liabilities. Distinguish ownership, access, possession, and authority.

29. Personal Belongings and Sentimental Items

Keepsakes, heirlooms, memorabilia, mementos, provenance, emotional or cultural significance, and current status. Distinguish sentimental belongings from operational equipment and financial assets.

Category IX — Security and Legal Matters

26. Legal and Criminal Record

Legal status, citizenship where relevant, criminal history, convictions, warrants, military discipline, legal disputes, jurisdiction, privileges, and immunities. An allegation is not a conviction.

54. Security and Personal Protection Profile

Personal security practices, threat awareness, protective details, bodyguards, defensive precautions, counter-surveillance, operational security, facility security, emergency protocols, vulnerabilities, and security history. Distinguish security capability from combat proficiency and intelligence expertise.

38. Secrets and Confidential Information

Personal secrets, hidden history, aliases or covert identities, classified information, restricted knowledge, hidden relationships or capabilities, who knows, disclosure, and confidentiality status. Distinguish a secret from information that is merely unknown or formally classified.

Category X — Health and Medical History

14. Medical and Physiological History

Diseases, injuries, surgeries, prosthetics, implants, treatments, chronic conditions, recovery, physiological conditions, and medical limitations. Coordinate with Species-Specific Characteristics and Physical Capabilities without duplicating their general reference material.

Category XI — Reputation and Communication

28. Reputation and Public Perception

General, species, political, military, factional, and community perceptions; recognition; conflicting public and private views; and reputation development. Reputation is contextual and not necessarily universal.

20. Voice and Communication

Vocal characteristics, pitch, tone, accent, dialect, languages, speech patterns, communication methods, nonverbal signals, translators, and communication limitations. Official performances are the primary reference where available. Do not invent unsupported vocal details.

39. Character Voice and Dialogue Reference

Dialogue style, vocabulary, expressions, humor, conversational habits, emotional speech, situational differences, representative dialogue, and writing guidance. Examples must be original demonstrations, not falsely attributed official quotations. Distinguish this writing reference from actual voice and communication characteristics.

Category XII — Omniversal and Continuity Information

16. Omniversal Status

Entry into and status within the Omniversal Realm, circumstances of arrival, acquired experiences and knowledge, Omniversal relationships, changes in status, and other relevant Omniversal developments. Do not assume a universal entry point or automatic knowledge of the Realm.

18. Ship Assignment

Whether the character is assigned to Azrael’s personal Mass Effect ship, the nature and duration of the assignment, role, responsibilities, and relevant changes. Assignment is individual and does not imply automatic crew membership.

19. Continuity and Canon Conflicts

Conflicts among games, DLC, supplementary sources, and player-choice branches; applicable source hierarchy; how conflicts are documented; and whether they remain unresolved. Do not force false reconciliation.

6. Cross-section boundaries and documentation discipline

Use these distinctions to prevent unnecessary duplication:

• Personality / Psychological Profile / Psychological and Behavioral Patterns: general temperament / internal psychological state / observable recurring behavior.
• Timeline and History / Notable Events and Appearances / Character Development / Character Arc: chronological events / source appearances / overall progression / pivotal transformations.
• Relationships / Family and Ancestry / Romantic and Intimate History / Social Network: individual connections / family context / romantic and intimate history / broader network.
• Abilities / Skills / Physical Capabilities / Combat Profile: what can be done / learned competencies / physical performance / application in combat.
• Professional and Civilian Life / Education and Training / Operational History: occupation and life context / preparation and qualifications / assignments and missions.
• Knowledge / Intelligence and Information Profile / Scientific and Technical Knowledge: what the character knows / how information is gathered and assessed / scientific expertise and research.
• Equipment / Resources and Personal Assets / Personal Belongings: operational gear / assets and resources / sentimental possessions.
• Biotic Profile / Technological Profile / Scientific and Technical Knowledge: biotic capabilities / technical interaction and expertise / scientific disciplines and research.
• Omniversal Status / Survival, Death, and Resurrection History: Omniversal circumstances and status / death and survival events in Mass Effect continuity. Cross-reference where necessary.
• Voice and Communication / Character Voice and Dialogue Reference: actual voice and communication traits / guidance for writing dialogue.

Use cross-references rather than reproducing extensive information in multiple sections. A brief summary may appear in more than one place when necessary for readability, but the detailed record should have one primary location.

7. Applying the standard

1. Retain all 55 section identifiers and titles.
2. Include all sections in a full profile, but do not force content into sections where it does not apply.
3. Mark missing or inapplicable information accurately.
4. Cite official sources close to the claims they support.
5. Identify framework additions and interpretations explicitly.
6. Preserve relevant player-dependent outcomes and composite timeline experiences.
7. Keep individual facts separate from species-wide or universe-wide framework rules.
8. Record changes and significant revisions when profiles are updated.
9. Cross-reference related sections to reduce duplication.
10. Do not infer ability, knowledge, beliefs, loyalty, compatibility, authority, or competence without support.
11. Preserve character autonomy regardless of faction, employment, relationship, or ship assignment.
12. Apply Omniversal rules only where relevant; do not allow Omniversal mechanics to silently rewrite Mass Effect continuity.

8. Revision policy

This standard is established following a full overlap and completeness audit. Its current structure contains 55 sections in 12 categories. No additional sections are required at this time.

Future changes should be deliberate and based on a demonstrated need. Prefer clarifying an existing section, adding a subsection, or refining cross-references over creating a new section that duplicates existing coverage.

The project lead controls promotion of project material into Master Canon. This document establishes the reusable Mass Effect profile standard; it does not independently establish the canon of any individual character.
